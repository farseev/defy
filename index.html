02 — The state of play

Your best people are doing the work they're worst suited to.

What are Defy Agents?

Defy Agents are five AI agents that each run a real marketing job from start to finish: paid media, CRM, influencer and GMV, strategy, and community management. They read live category signal from Defy Monitor, do the work, and hand back something a team can approve and ship — a scored ad set, a built email flow, a per-creator brief, a client-ready deck, a week of posts. Agencies license them per client and white-label them. Every agent has a human sign-off gate before anything spends money or goes public.

We pulled the audience research on marketing leads across our own listening workspaces. The same four tensions came back every time: tools that don't talk to each other, research that arrives after the decision, quiet distrust of anything automated, and the slow cost of doing nothing about it. None of that is solved by another dashboard.

How the work goes now

  • Three weeks of desk research, and the deck lands after the client has already moved.
  • Creative gets tested by spending money on it, then read backwards from the result.
  • Subject lines chosen by whoever argued hardest in the meeting.
  • One note to the whole creator roster: post more, tag us.
  • Six tools that don't speak to each other, stitched together by a strategist at 9pm.

How it goes with an agent on it

  • A month of desk research produced in one run, rendered in the client's own brand.
  • Every new ad scored for likely click-through before the budget moves.
  • Three genuinely different email strategies, and the winner called at 95% significance.
  • A bespoke brief per creator, evidenced with that creator's own posts.
  • One signal layer underneath all five agents, refreshed continuously.
03 — The agents

01

Paid Media Agent

Score the ad before you spend the budget.
Live
How it works
  1. Comes in a few days into a live Meta campaign, with enough delivery data to read and before fatigue sets in.
  2. Works out which live ads are actually winning for the campaign's goal. Awareness, traffic, leads and sales each change what "good" means, and it treats them differently. It also flags what's fatiguing or too alike.
  3. Extracts the concept behind the winners and finds where the category's paid whitespace sits.
  4. Generates a fresh funnel of on-brand ads in that space: top, middle and bottom.
  5. Scores every new ad for likely click-through before a penny is spent, and flags the strongest.
  6. Pushes the top scorers back into the same campaign, mirroring the winning ad's page, copy and CTA. They arrive paused, waiting for you.
  7. Later, checks what it predicted against what actually happened, and feeds that back in.
What makes it different It calls performance before the money goes in. That means skipping whole rounds of multivariate media testing and only ever putting spend behind creative the model already rates a winner. The prediction is shown as a prediction, and it gets closed out honestly against the real result.
Who it's for
  • Signs it offAgency MD or Head of Performance. The P&L on media efficiency sits with them. Brand-side, the Performance Marketing Director.
  • Champions itThe paid lead. Their win-rate goes up and the testing budget they have to defend goes down.
  • Also in the roomCreative, who stop making throwaway variants. The account lead, who gets better numbers to walk into the review with. Finance, who see the media efficiency.
The objection we hear
“A model isn't going to beat my buyer's instinct.”
It isn't trying to. It kills the obvious losers so your buyer spends their judgment on the close calls, which is where that judgment was always worth the most. The approval gate before activation is not optional, because this agent touches real budget.
Proof
Already run end to end on a real Meta account: diagnosed the campaign, generated the funnel, scored it, pushed it and activated.
02

CRM Agent

Know the winning email before you hit send.
In final build
How it works
  1. You give it a target audience and a goal.
  2. It designs three genuinely different email strategies, varying message, offer, frequency and channel. Not three versions of the same idea with one word changed. It explains the thinking behind each.
  3. You approve the strategy. That's gate one.
  4. It builds the approved variants as live assets inside Klaviyo: flows, audience splits, copy, schedules, frequency caps.
  5. You approve the built assets. That's gate two.
  6. It goes live. Klaviyo splits and sends. The agent watches.
  7. It calls the statistically significant winner at 95% on a minimum sample. The winner becomes the new control, fresh challengers are generated, and it loops, building a memory of what works for that audience.
What makes it different It works out the winning message before the send, then keeps optimising on its own with human sign-off at both gates. A kill switch stops the loop inside a minute. Platform-agnostic by design; Klaviyo is simply the first integration we finished.
Who it's for
  • Signs it offHead of CRM or Retention, or the Ecommerce Director. Agency-side, the CRM practice lead.
  • Champions itThe CRM or lifecycle manager, who is tired of guessing subject lines and running tests by hand.
  • Also in the roomEmail ops, who stop hand-building variants. Analytics, who stop being asked to call winners manually.
The objection we hear
“I'm not letting an AI write into my live sends.”
Fair. Nothing goes live without two separate human approvals, and the whole loop stops in under a minute if you want it to. IT, deliverability and the CRM manager each tend to raise a different version of this objection, and the two gates plus the kill switch are the answer to all three.
Where it stands
The engine is built and the technical quote is done. Final build sign-off is still open, so this is the one to plan for next, not now. We'd rather tell you that than sell it as shipped.
03

Influencer & GMV Agent

Brief every creator on exactly what lifts sales.
Live
How it works
  1. Starts with your roster of sales-driving creators on TikTok Shop.
  2. Gives each creator a distinct lane to own, so no two are competing on the same ground.
  3. For each one, works out what's actually driving their sales: what they say, how they present the product, how they frame the hook.
  4. Returns a bespoke brief per creator on exactly what to change to lift engagement and sales, with that creator's real posts as the evidence.
  5. Rolls it all up into a portfolio view across the roster, plus one deck per creator.
What makes it different Every creator gets an individual brief backed by their own numbers, rather than one generic "post more" note sent to forty people. Sales figures come from your programme export. The agent reads content and engagement, not the till, so the two sources stay labelled separately on every output.
Who it's for
  • Signs it offHead of Influencer or Social Commerce, or Brand Partnerships. Agency-side, the influencer practice lead.
  • Champions itThe creator manager, who wants per-creator direction instead of roster averages.
  • Also in the roomThe creators themselves, who finally get specific guidance. And whoever owns the GMV number.
The objection we hear
“My creators won't take notes from an algorithm.”
They don't have to. The brief is ammunition their manager delivers, in the manager's own voice, in a relationship that already exists. It makes that conversation sharper. It doesn't replace it, and talent managers who assume otherwise usually change their mind on the first brief.
Proof
Delivered on a live SAMY and L'Oréal creator programme.
04

Strategy Agent

A strategist's month of research, in a single run.
Live
How it works
  1. Reads the competitive field and the live audience conversation in the category.
  2. Finds the whitespace nobody owns. It will also rule an angle out, which matters more than it sounds: a research tool that only ever confirms is not research.
  3. Builds the whole picture: the target persona, the cultural tension to lean into, the core idea, and the creative concepts and messaging routes worth testing.
  4. Renders it as a client-ready deck in the client's own brand. A full briefing when the brand needs the rigour, or a tighter single-idea pitch for a new-business meeting.
What makes it different Weeks of a strategist's desk research, grounded in real category data rather than assumption, produced in one run and delivered as a brief a team can take straight into production. It is a starting brief, not a finished strategy, and we sell it that way on purpose.
Who it's for
  • Signs it offAgency MD or Strategy Director. Brand-side, the marketing lead commissioning the work.
  • Champions itThe new-business lead. Faster, better-grounded pitches win more accounts, and they feel that within a quarter.
  • Also in the roomAccount teams, who get a real brief to sell. Creative, who get a brief to build from.
The objection we hear
“Strategy is our IP. This commoditises it.”
It commoditises the desk research, which was never the IP. The planner still owns the judgment, the leap and the sell. What changes is that they stop spending three weeks assembling the floor they think from.
Proof
Produced for real clients across finance, healthcare, mobility and payments.
05

Community Management Agent

A daily, on-brand presence that runs itself.
Live · peer-reviewed
How it works
  1. Watches what's actually landing in the category through Defy Monitor, and breaks the best content down into angles, tensions and themes.
  2. Finds the story on its own, writes the post in the account's voice, and generates a brand-aligned image under hard constraints. No faces, no text, whatever the brand's rules are.
  3. Compiles and publishes to LinkedIn, and builds the calendar, schedules and posts across social.
  4. Runs the whole loop with nobody in it, or with a checkpoint if the brand wants one.
What makes it different A daily presence grounded in what is working in the category this week, rather than a scheduler full of evergreen posts nobody reads. The same engine also scales executive personal branding, which is how most founders end up using it first.

Then don't. It runs fully autonomous or behind a sign-off gate, and the brand picks. "No human in the loop" excites some buyers and frightens others, so brand-safety-sensitive clients start with the checkpoint and turn it off when they stop looking.

04 — What's underneath

Agents are only as good as what they're reading.

An agent working from assumption is a very fast way to be confidently wrong. All five sit on the same live category signal, and that layer is the part most people underestimate until they've used it.

Defy Monitor

Live competitor, audience and conversation intelligence on every client and category you work in. Every public ad and organic post decoded into proposition, persona, emotion, format and call to action, then scored and clustered. Refreshed continuously, not quarterly.

Defy MCP

Interrogate all of it from inside Claude and other MCP-compatible tools. Your team prompts it like a colleague. There's no dashboard to learn, which is usually the reason intelligence tools go quiet after month two.

The agents

Five jobs, run end to end on that signal. They subscribe to Monitor rather than replacing it, and each one hands its output to a human before anything spends, sends or publishes.

08 — Questions

The things people actually ask.

Who buys this, the agency or the brand?
Usually the agency. Defy licenses each agent to an agency partner per client, per month, on a twelve-month term, on top of a Defy Monitor subscription. The agency white-labels it and passes it to the client with its own margin on top. Brands with in-house teams can license directly, and some do, but the model was built for agencies first.
Does the Paid Media Agent spend my budget on its own?
No. It builds the new ads paused inside the same campaign and waits for a human. It reads the live campaign, works out which ads are winning against the goal, generates a fresh funnel in the category whitespace, and scores every new ad for likely click-through before anything moves. The approval gate before activation is not negotiable, precisely because this is the agent that touches money.
Will an AI write directly into my live Klaviyo account?
Only after two separate human approvals. The CRM Agent designs three genuinely different email strategies and you approve the strategy before it builds anything. It then builds flows, splits, copy, schedules and frequency caps inside Klaviyo, and you approve those built assets before they go live. A kill switch stops the loop inside a minute. The agent is platform-agnostic by design; Klaviyo is simply the first integration we finished.
How is this different from a social listening tool?
Listening tells you what was said. Monitor tells you what worked, and the agents act on it. Every competitor ad and organic post gets decoded into proposition, persona, emotion, format and call to action, then scored. What comes out the other end is a scored ad set, a built email flow, a per-creator brief or a client-ready deck. Not a sentiment dashboard.
Does this replace my planners, buyers and community managers?
It replaces the grind, not the judgment. The Strategy Agent does the desk research a planner would otherwise spend weeks on, and the planner still owns the thinking and the sell. The Paid Media Agent kills the obvious losers so a buyer spends judgment on the close calls. In practice the teams that adopt it fastest are the ones already drowning in the repetitive half of the job.
Can we run it entirely in our own brand?
Yes. The dashboard and the agent outputs are white-labelled: your subdomain, your logo and colours, your team and clients seated. The Strategy Agent goes further and renders its decks in the end client's brand, so what lands in the client's inbox looks like it came from you, because it did.
Which agent should we start with?
Community Management if you want the lowest-friction start, because there's no media budget and no live sends involved. Paid Media if you want the sharpest proof, because it touches the number the client cares most about. Both are live today.
Is the CRM Agent available now?
The engine is built and the technical quote is done. Final build sign-off is still open, so it's the one to plan for next quarter rather than this one. We'd rather say that plainly than sell you something as shipped when it isn't.
Where do the sales numbers in the creator briefs come from?
From your own programme export. The Influencer & GMV Agent reads content and engagement, not the till, so GMV and earnings figures stay labelled as coming from your data on every slide. Mixing the two would make the briefs look more certain than they are.
What does it cost?
It depends on the agent, the number of client accounts and where Monitor sits underneath it. We'll walk you through the licensing on a call rather than putting a number on a page that turns out to be wrong for your setup. Email us and we'll come back with something specific.
